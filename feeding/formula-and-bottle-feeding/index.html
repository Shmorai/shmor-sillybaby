Choosing formula

  • First infant formula (usually cows' milk based) is suitable for the whole first year. The NHS says follow-on formula isn't needed, and toddler or growing-up milks aren't necessary after 12 months.
  • All infant formula sold in the UK and EU must meet the same legal composition rules, and US formula must meet FDA requirements, so pricier brands aren't nutritionally “better”.
  • Only use specialist formulas (for example, for cows' milk allergy) on the advice of a health professional.
  • Never water down formula or add anything to the bottle.

How to make up a formula feed (UK NHS method)

Powdered formula isn't sterile, so the NHS advises using water at at least 70°C to kill any bacteria:

  1. Fill the kettle with at least 1 litre of fresh tap water, boil it, then leave it to cool for no more than 30 minutes.
  2. Clean and disinfect the work surface; wash your hands.
  3. Pour the correct amount of water into a sterilised bottle (check the formula pack).
  4. Add the exact number of level scoops, using the scoop in the pack.
  5. Put on the teat and cap, and shake well.
  6. Cool quickly under a cold tap, and test on the inside of your wrist: it should feel warm, not hot.
  7. Throw away any formula left after a feed.

Never warm bottles in a microwave (it creates hot spots). Ready-to-feed liquid formula is sterile and doesn't need hot water, which is useful for nights and travel. In the US, the CDC and the formula label give the instructions to follow; ask your paediatrician what's right for your baby.

Sterilising bottles

Sterilise bottles, teats and equipment until your baby is at least 12 months old (NHS). Wash in hot soapy water first, then use a steam steriliser, boiling (10 minutes) or cold-water sterilising solution, following the maker's instructions.

Paced, responsive bottle feeding

Hold close and upright

Keep your baby fairly upright, supporting the head, and look at them.

Invite the teat

Brush the teat on their lips and let them draw it in.

Bottle horizontal

Tip it just enough to fill the teat, so they control the pace.

Pause and watch

Give breaks and stop when they show fullness cues; no need to finish the bottle.

Never prop a bottle or leave a baby alone to feed. Limiting the number of people who feed a young baby helps build closeness.

Frequently asked questions

How much formula does a baby need?
It varies by baby and age; the formula pack gives a guide. Follow your baby’s hunger and fullness cues rather than forcing them to finish a bottle, and ask your health visitor or paediatrician if you’re unsure about amounts or weight gain.
Can I make up bottles in advance?
The NHS advises making up each feed fresh. If you need to prepare a feed in advance (for example, at nursery), cool it quickly and store it in the back of the fridge for no more than 24 hours. Ready-to-feed formula is a convenient alternative.
Do I need follow-on formula at 6 months?
No. The NHS says first infant formula can be used for the whole first year, and follow-on formula isn’t needed. From 12 months, whole cow’s milk can be the main drink.
