Sleep bags or light blanketsWarmth without loose beddingCorrect size; fitted neck, no hood
Nappies and wipesYou’ll use plentyRight size band

Prams, pushchairs and strollers

Newborns need to lie flat, so choose a pram, carrycot or a pushchair that reclines fully. Think about where you will use it (pavements, parks, public transport), how it folds, how much it weighs and whether it fits your car boot. Look for EN 1888 (UK/EU) or ASTM F833 (US) on the label, and always use the harness and brakes.

Baby carriers and slings

Carriers keep your baby close and your hands free. Follow the T.I.C.K.S. sling safety rules: Tight, In view at all times, Close enough to kiss, Keep chin off the chest, Supported back. Choose a carrier that supports your baby's hips in an “M” position (knees higher than bottom).

Things you can skip (or wait on)

  • Cot bumpers, sleep positioners, wedges and pillows: not recommended for safe sleep.
  • Baby walkers: the AAP advises against them because of injury risk.
  • Bottle prep gadgets, wipe warmers, special laundry detergents: nice to have at most.
  • Lots of newborn clothes: babies outgrow them in weeks.

Second-hand? Great for clothes, toys and many bigger items. Avoid second-hand car seats unless you know the full history, and buy a new mattress for each baby if you can (The Lullaby Trust).

Frequently asked questions

What baby items do I need before birth?
At minimum: a rear-facing car seat (if you’ll travel by car), a safe sleep space with a firm, flat mattress, nappies, a few clothes and sleep bags, and feeding basics. Most other things can wait until you know what you need.
Is it safe to buy second-hand baby gear?
Many items are fine second-hand if they’re in good condition and meet current safety standards. Avoid second-hand car seats unless you know their full history, check for recalls, and the Lullaby Trust suggests a new mattress for each baby if possible.
