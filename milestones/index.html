Milestones: the big firsts

Smiles, rolls, first steps, first words. Here is what most children do by each age, how to encourage it, and when to ask for a check.

Photo: Brooke Balentine on Unsplash

Written by the SillyBaby editorial team · Checked against NHS, CDC and AAP guidance · Updated 18 November 2026 · Editorial standards

Milestones timeline

These are things most children (about 3 in 4) can do by each age, from the CDC's updated checklists. Use them as a guide for conversations with your health visitor or paediatrician, not as a test.

  • 2 months

    Smiles when you talk to or smile at them · holds head up when on tummy · looks at your face · makes sounds other than crying.

  • 4 months

    Holds head steady without support when held · brings hands to mouth · coos · chuckles when you try to make them laugh.

  • 6 months

    Rolls from tummy to back · laughs · reaches to grab a toy · leans on hands when sitting.

  • 9 months

    Sits without support · babbles “mamamama”, “bababababa” · looks for things you drop · may be shy with strangers.

  • 12 months

    Pulls up to stand · walks holding on to furniture · waves bye-bye · uses “mama”, “dada” or a special name.

  • 18 months

    Walks without holding on · tries to say three or more words · scribbles · follows one-step directions.

  • 2 years

    Kicks a ball · runs · says two words together · points to things in a book.

  • 3 years

    Talks in short conversations · puts on some clothes · draws a circle when shown how.

  • 4 years

    Says sentences of four or more words · catches a large ball most of the time · pretends in play.

  • 5 years

    Tells a short story · counts to 10 · hops on one foot · takes turns in games.

How to help, at any age

Talk, talk, talk

Chat about what you’re doing and respond to their sounds and words.

Floor time

Tummy time for babies; space to move, climb and explore later.

Read together

A few minutes of books every day builds language.

Play

Simple toys, songs and games beat screens for learning.

Ideas by age in play and learning. The CDC's free Milestone Tracker app lets you tick off milestones and share them with your doctor.

When to talk to a professional

Act early if you have concerns. Speak to your health visitor, GP or paediatrician if your child:

  • is not reaching several milestones for their age,
  • loses skills they once had (at any age),
  • doesn't respond to sounds or seem to see things,
  • has stiff or very floppy muscles, or uses one side of the body much more than the other.

In the UK, your health visitor will review development at around 1 year and 2 to 2½ years. In the US, the AAP recommends developmental screening at the 9, 18 and 30-month well-child visits and autism screening at 18 and 24 months.

Last updated: 18 November 2026

Frequently asked questions

What if my baby misses a milestone?
Children develop at different rates, and missing one milestone by a little is often fine. But it’s always OK to ask: speak to your health visitor, GP or paediatrician, especially if your child misses several milestones or loses skills.
Do premature babies reach milestones later?
Often, yes. Health professionals usually use corrected age (from your baby’s due date) when checking development for premature babies, typically until about age 2.
When do babies say their first word?
Many babies say a first word such as “mama” or “dada” with meaning around their first birthday. By 18 months, the CDC lists trying to say three or more words besides “mama” or “dada”.
