Emergency, urgent or routine?

Call nowGet advice todayMention at the next check
999 (UK) · 911 (US) · 112 (EU) if your child is struggling to breathe, turns blue or grey, is floppy or unresponsive, has a fit (seizure) for the first time, has a rash that doesn’t fade under a glass, or has a severe allergic reaction.Call NHS 111, your GP or your paediatrician if a baby under 3 months has a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or more, your child is not feeding or drinking, has fewer wet nappies, or you’re worried.Questions about growth, development, sleep, feeding or behaviour that aren’t urgent: your health visitor or well-child visit.

Trust your instincts. You know your child best. If something feels wrong, get help.

Vaccinations

Vaccines protect babies and children from serious illnesses. Each country has its own official schedule, which changes from time to time, so always use the current one: the NHS vaccination schedule in the UK, the CDC immunization schedule in the US, or your national health service in Europe. In the UK, the first routine vaccines are offered at 8 weeks; your GP surgery will invite you. A temperature after vaccines is common: see fever.

First aid basics worth learning

Choking

Back blows and chest thrusts (babies) or abdominal thrusts (over 1), then call for help.

CPR

Baby and child CPR differs from adult CPR. A short course builds confidence.

Burns and scalds

Cool under cool running water for 20 minutes, then get it checked.

Poisoning

Call 111 / your doctor, or Poison Control in the US (1-800-222-1222).

Free and low-cost courses and guides come from the British Red Cross, St John Ambulance and the American Red Cross.

Frequently asked questions

When should I call NHS 111 about my baby?
Call NHS 111 (UK) if you’re worried and it isn’t an emergency: for example, a high temperature, fewer wet nappies, not feeding, or a rash you’re unsure about. Call 999 for emergencies such as breathing difficulty, a fit or a rash that doesn’t fade under a glass.
Where can I find the vaccine schedule?
Use your country’s current official schedule: the NHS vaccination schedule in the UK and the CDC child and adolescent immunization schedule in the US. Your GP surgery or paediatrician will tell you what your child is due.
