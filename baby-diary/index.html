Daily log: feeds, nappies and sleep

Use one sheet a day in the early weeks. Tick W for a wet nappy and D for a dirty one.

TimeFeed (side / amount)Nappy W / DSleep from–toMoodNotes

From around day 5, the NHS says a well-fed baby has at least 6 heavy wet nappies a day. Fewer than that, or no poo, is worth mentioning to your midwife or health visitor.

Diary prompts, month by month

  • Month 1

    How did you choose their name? Who visited first? What surprised you most?

  • Month 3

    What makes them smile? Favourite song or cuddle position?

  • Month 6

    First tastes and funniest food faces. New sounds they make.

  • Month 9

    Favourite toy, game or book. Where do they love to go?

  • Month 12

    First birthday: who came, what they ate, how far they have come.

  • Every year

    A height mark on the door frame, a favourite saying, a drawing they made.

Prefer an app? In the UK, your baby's red book (Personal Child Health Record) is also increasingly available digitally in some areas; the CDC's Milestone Tracker app is free in the US.

Questions to take to appointments

  • Is my baby gaining weight as expected?
  • Is this many feeds, wet and dirty nappies normal for their age?
  • Which vaccines are next, and when?
  • What should I do if they get a temperature?
  • How am I doing? (Your wellbeing matters too.)

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to track my newborn’s feeds?
It isn’t required, but many parents find a simple log helpful in the first weeks, especially if there are questions about feeding or weight. Your midwife or health visitor may ask about wet and dirty nappies.
