Safe sleep in one picture

The US “ABCs” sum it up: babies sleep Alone (in their own space), on their Back, in a Crib (or cot or bassinet).

Do this, every sleep

  • On the back for every sleep, day and night, until your baby can roll both ways on their own.
  • A clear, flat, firm sleep space: a cot, crib, Moses basket or bassinet with a firm, flat, waterproof mattress, and nothing else in it (no pillows, quilts, bumpers, positioners or soft toys).
  • Same room as you for at least the first 6 months, in their own sleep space.
  • Feet to foot: if using a blanket, place your baby's feet at the foot of the cot and tuck a thin blanket no higher than the shoulders. A well-fitting baby sleep bag is a simple alternative.
  • Not too hot: a room temperature of 16 to 20°C is comfortable. Check by feeling your baby's chest or back of the neck; keep their head uncovered and remove hats indoors.
  • Smoke-free: keep your baby smoke-free during pregnancy and after birth.
  • Breastfeeding, if you can, lowers the risk.

Avoid these

Sofas and armchairs

Never fall asleep with your baby on a sofa or armchair. If you might fall asleep, put your baby in their cot.

Bed-sharing risks

Never share a bed if you or your partner smoke, have drunk alcohol, taken drugs or medicine that makes you drowsy, or your baby was premature or low birth weight.

Sleeping in car seats

Car seats, swings and bouncers aren’t for sleep at home. Move your baby to a flat sleep space when you can.

Weighted and inclined products

The AAP advises against weighted sleep sacks, swaddles and blankets, and inclined sleepers.

Sleep bags, swaddling and TOG

A baby sleep bag (also called a sleep sack or wearable blanket) stops covers ending up over your baby's face. Choose one that is the right size for your baby's weight, fits well around the neck and armholes so they can't slip inside, has no hood, and has a TOG (warmth) rating suited to your room. Our post TOG ratings explained has more.

If you swaddle, use a thin material, don't wrap above the shoulders, keep it loose around the hips so the legs can bend up and out, always place your baby on their back, and stop swaddling as soon as your baby shows signs of trying to roll. When your baby is unwell

Babies who are unwell may need closer watching. Get urgent medical help if your baby stops breathing, turns blue, is floppy or unresponsive: call 999 (UK), 911 (US) or 112 (EU). A baby first aid course (from the Red Cross, St John Ambulance or your local hospital) is time well spent.

Frequently asked questions

When can babies sleep on their tummy?
Always put your baby down on their back. Once your baby can roll from back to front and back again on their own, you don’t need to keep turning them back, but keep putting them down on their back and keep the cot clear.
What room temperature is best for a baby?
The Lullaby Trust advises a room temperature of 16 to 20°C (about 61 to 68°F), with light bedding or a lightweight, well-fitting sleep bag. Feel your baby’s chest or the back of their neck to check they’re not too hot.
How long should my baby sleep in my room?
The Lullaby Trust, NHS and AAP all advise your baby sleeps in a cot or crib in the same room as you for at least the first 6 months.
Are cot bumpers safe?
No. Safe sleep organisations advise against cot bumpers, pillows, quilts, positioners and soft toys in the sleep space.
