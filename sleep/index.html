How much sleep does my child need?

Recommended total sleep in 24 hours (including naps for under-6s). These are ranges: plenty of healthy children sit at either end.

Safe sleep comes first

For babies, how they sleep matters even more than how long. Always on the back, in a clear, flat sleep space, in your room for at least the first 6 months. Read our safe sleep guide and see the diagram.

Building a bedtime routine

  • Wind down

    Dim lights and quieter play about 30 minutes before bed.

  • Bath or wash

    A warm bath or wash and fresh nappy or pyjamas.

  • Teeth

    Brush with fluoride toothpaste once teeth arrive.

  • Books and songs

    The same few steps every night help children know sleep is coming.

  • Into bed

    Ideally drowsy but awake, so they learn to settle in their own space.

Night waking and naps

Young babies need night feeds, and night waking remains common well into the toddler years. Developmental leaps, teething, illness and changes like starting nursery can all unsettle sleep for a while. Most babies take several naps a day, moving to two naps in the second half of the first year and one nap during the second year; many children stop napping between 3 and 5.

If sleep problems are affecting your family, talk to your health visitor or paediatrician. Snoring with pauses in breathing should always be checked by a doctor.

Frequently asked questions

When will my baby sleep through the night?
There’s no set age. Many babies still wake at night in the first year, and that’s normal. Feeding at night is important for young babies. Talk to your health visitor or paediatrician if you’re struggling.
How many naps does a 1 year old need?
Most 1 year olds have one or two naps a day, and most move to a single nap during their second year. The AASM recommends 11 to 14 hours of total sleep in 24 hours for 1 to 2 year olds.
