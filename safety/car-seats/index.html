Car seats, stage by stage

A correctly fitted car seat is one of the most important things you will buy. Here are the stages, the rules and the checks that matter.

The four stages

Rules in the UK, Europe and the US

WhereMain rules
UKChildren must use a child car seat until they are 12 years old or 135cm tall, whichever comes first. Only EU-approved seats (R44 or R129) can be used. Never fit a rear-facing seat in front of an active airbag. Under R129 (i-Size), babies stay rear-facing until at least 15 months.
EUEU law requires children under 150cm to use an approved child restraint (some countries set 135cm). New seats are approved to UN R129 (i-Size).
USLaws vary by state. The AAP advises rear-facing for as long as possible, until the highest height or weight the seat allows, then a forward-facing harness, then a belt-positioning booster until the seat belt fits (usually at about 4 ft 9 in / 145cm, between ages 8 and 12). Children under 13 should ride in the back seat. Seats must meet FMVSS 213.

Fitting checks every time

  • Tight install: the seat should move no more than about an inch (2.5cm) side to side at the belt path or ISOFIX base.
  • Harness: snug enough that you can't pinch any slack at the shoulder (the “pinch test”). Chest clip at armpit level (US seats).
  • No bulky coats: take off puffy coats and snowsuits before buckling; tuck a blanket over the harness instead.
  • Recline: newborns need the correct recline angle so their head doesn't flop forward.
  • Not for sleep at home: move your baby to a flat sleep space after the journey. Many manufacturers also advise regular breaks on long trips.

Second-hand seats and getting a check

Avoid second-hand car seats unless you know their full history: a seat that has been in a crash may be damaged in ways you can't see, and seats have expiry dates in the US. Free fitting help: in the US, a certified Child Passenger Safety Technician via NHTSA; in the UK, many retailers offer fitting checks, and childcarseats.org.uk (RoSPA) explains the rules.

Frequently asked questions

When can my baby face forward in the car?
Keep your child rear-facing for as long as their seat allows. In the UK and EU, R129 (i-Size) seats require rear-facing until at least 15 months. The AAP advises staying rear-facing until your child reaches the seat’s rear-facing height or weight limit, which for many seats is age 2 or older.
When can a child stop using a booster seat in the UK?
UK law says children must use a child car seat until they are 12 years old or 135cm tall, whichever comes first. Many safety experts recommend using a booster until 150cm.
Can my baby wear a coat in a car seat?
Take off thick or puffy coats and snowsuits before buckling, because they can stop the harness fitting tightly. Use thin layers and put a blanket or coat over the top of the harness instead.
