Colic: signs, soothing and coping

Hours of crying you can’t seem to settle is exhausting and upsetting. Colic is common, it doesn’t last forever, and you are not doing anything wrong.

By the SillyBaby editorial team · 16 November 2026 · Updated 18 November 2026 · 2 min read

What is colic?

Colic is when a healthy baby cries a lot without an obvious reason. The NHS says it is common in the first few weeks and usually stops by around 3 to 4 months (sometimes 6 months). Crying in all babies tends to peak at around 6 to 8 weeks.

Signs of colic

  • Intense crying for more than 3 hours a day, 3 days a week, for at least a week
  • Crying that's often in the late afternoon or evening
  • Hard to soothe or settle
  • Clenched fists, a red face, arching the back, or bringing the knees up to the tummy

Soothing ideas to try

Hold and move

Hold your baby close, rock, sway or walk with them, or use a sling.

White noise

Gentle shushing, a fan or calm background sound.

Warm bath

Some babies relax in a warm (not hot) bath.

Feeding checks

Wind during and after feeds; ask about bottle position or latch.

Fresh air

A walk in the pram or carrier.

Swap with someone

Tag-team with a partner, family or friend.

The NHS says there is little evidence for most colic remedies sold in shops, so speak to a pharmacist or health visitor before buying anything. Don't change your baby's formula without advice.

Coping when the crying won’t stop

It is completely normal to feel frustrated. If you feel overwhelmed, put your baby down safely on their back in their cot, close the door, and take a few minutes to calm down. Never shake a baby; it can cause serious brain injury. The UK's ICON programme has practical tips. Cry-sis (UK) offers a helpline for parents of crying babies.

When to see a doctor

Get medical advice if your baby's cry sounds different (high-pitched or weak), they have a temperature of 38°C or higher (under 3 months), they're not feeding or have fewer wet nappies, they vomit green, have blood in their poo, or seem floppy or unwell. Call 999 / 911 if they have trouble breathing or are unresponsive.

Frequently asked questions

When does colic stop?
Colic usually improves by 3 to 4 months, and almost always by 6 months, according to the NHS.
Can breastfeeding mums change their diet to help colic?
Sometimes a health professional may suggest cutting out certain foods if there’s a sign of allergy, but don’t make big changes without advice. Talk to your GP, health visitor or paediatrician.
