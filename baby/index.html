Development: rolling, sitting, crawling

Based on the CDC's milestone checklists, most babies:

  • 4 months

    Hold their head steady without support when held, bring hands to mouth, coo and make sounds back to you, and chuckle when you try to make them laugh.

  • 6 months

    Roll from tummy to back, laugh, reach to grab a toy, and lean on their hands to support themselves when sitting.

  • 9 months

    Sit without support, make sounds like “mamamama” and “bababababa”, look for things you drop, and may be shy or clingy with strangers.

  • 12 months

    Pull up to stand, walk holding on to furniture, wave bye-bye, call a parent “mama”, “dada” or a special name, and pick up small things between thumb and finger.

Some babies crawl, some bottom-shuffle and some skip crawling altogether. See the full milestones timeline.

Feeding: milk and starting solids

Breast milk or first infant formula stays the main drink for the whole first year. At around 6 months your baby can start solid foods (weaning) when they show all three signs of readiness: sitting up and holding their head steady, coordinating eyes, hands and mouth, and swallowing food rather than pushing it out. The NHS advises not starting before 17 weeks.

Offer a variety of foods, including common allergens such as egg and peanut (in a smooth, age-suitable form), one at a time. No honey before 12 months, no added salt or sugar, and no whole nuts until age 5. Full guide: starting solids.

From about 6 months, offer sips of water from an open or free-flow cup with meals.

Sleep from 3 to 12 months

Many babies start to sleep for longer stretches at night, but waking is still normal. The American Academy of Sleep Medicine recommends 12 to 16 hours in 24 hours (including naps) for babies aged 4 to 12 months. Keep following safe sleep advice: on the back, clear cot, same room for at least the first 6 months. Once your baby can roll both ways by themselves, you don't need to roll them back. Stop swaddling once they show signs of trying to roll.

Teeth, health and safety

First teeth often appear at around 6 months, but anywhere in the first year is normal. Start brushing twice a day as soon as a tooth appears, using a smear of fluoride toothpaste. See teething.

Once your baby is on the move, it is time to childproof: stair gates, anchored furniture, cords and button batteries out of reach, and hot drinks away from the edge. Keep your baby rear-facing in the car for as long as their seat allows (car seats).

Babies 3 to 6 months old with a temperature of 39°C (102.2°F) or higher should be seen urgently (NHS). See fever in children.

Play ideas that build skills

Tummy time games

Lie face to face, use a mirror or put a toy just out of reach.

Talk and sing

Narrate your day, copy their babble and pause for their “reply”.

Peekaboo

Teaches that things still exist when hidden (object permanence).

Books

Board books with faces and bright pictures, shared every day.

Frequently asked questions

When do babies start crawling?
Many babies start crawling between about 6 and 10 months, but some shuffle on their bottoms instead and some go straight to walking. What matters more is that your baby is using both sides of their body and gaining new skills. Ask your health visitor or paediatrician if you are worried.
When can my baby start solid food?
Around 6 months, when they can sit and hold their head steady, coordinate eyes, hands and mouth, and swallow food. The NHS advises not before 17 weeks. Talk to your health professional if your baby was premature or has a medical condition.
Do babies need water?
Babies under 6 months who are breastfed or formula fed don’t need water. From around 6 months you can offer sips of water from a cup with meals.
