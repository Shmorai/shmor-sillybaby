Teething: tiny teeth, big feelings

Dribble, chewing everything, red cheeks and the odd grumpy night. Here is what helps sore gums, what to avoid and how to look after those first teeth.

When do babies start teething?

The NHS says some babies are born with their first teeth, while others start teething before 4 months and some after 12 months, but most start at around 6 months. Most children have all 20 baby teeth by about 2½ years.

Signs of teething

  • Sore, red gums where the tooth is coming through
  • A mild temperature (less than 38°C) in some babies
  • One flushed cheek, dribbling, gnawing and chewing
  • Being more fretful than usual and not sleeping well

Teething doesn't cause a high temperature (38°C or more) or diarrhoea. If your baby has these, look for another cause and get advice. See fever.

What helps

Something to chew

A clean, solid teething ring cooled in the fridge (not the freezer).

Gum rub

Gently rub the gum with a clean finger.

Healthy snacks

For babies over 6 months: chilled cucumber or carrot sticks, supervised.

Comfort

Cuddles, distraction and extra patience.

If your baby is in pain, ask a pharmacist about sugar-free paracetamol or ibuprofen suitable for their age.

What to avoid

  • Amber teething necklaces, bracelets and anklets: the FDA warns they can cause strangulation and choking.
  • Benzocaine teething gels: the FDA advises against them for children under 2. In the UK, ask a pharmacist before using any teething gel.
  • Homeopathic teething tablets: the FDA has warned against them.
  • Frozen teething rings, which can damage gums.

Brushing first teeth

Start brushing as soon as the first tooth appears, twice a day (last thing at night and one other time). NHS advice: under 3s, a smear of toothpaste with at least 1,000ppm fluoride; 3 to 6 year olds, a pea-sized amount with 1,000 to 1,500ppm. Spit, don't rinse. Take your child to the dentist when the first teeth appear (NHS dental care is free for under-18s in the UK); the AAP and American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry advise a first dental visit by the first birthday.

Frequently asked questions

Does teething cause a fever?
Teething can cause a slightly raised temperature in some babies, but the NHS says a high temperature (38°C or more) isn’t caused by teething. If your baby has a fever, check for other causes and get advice if you’re worried.
Are amber teething necklaces safe?
No. The US FDA warns that teething necklaces, bracelets and anklets can cause strangulation and choking, and there’s no good evidence they work.
When should my baby first see a dentist?
The NHS suggests taking your child to the dentist when their first teeth appear. The AAP and American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommend a first dental visit by the first birthday.
