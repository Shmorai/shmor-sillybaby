Potty training, the calm way

There is no prize for being first. Waiting until your child is ready usually makes potty training quicker and happier for everyone.

Signs your toddler is ready

Knows they’re going

Tells you, or hides, while weeing or pooing.

Longer dry spells

Stays dry for an hour or two.

Notices nappies

Pulls at a wet or dirty nappy, or says they don’t like it.

Can follow steps

Understands simple instructions and can pull trousers down and up with help.

Many children show these signs between about 18 months and 3 years. Every child is different.

A step-by-step plan

  1. Talk about it: read potty books, let them watch you or siblings, and keep the potty where they can see it.
  2. Pick a calm time, not during a big change like a new sibling or moving house.
  3. Easy clothes: elastic waists, no fiddly buttons.
  4. Regular sits: after meals, after naps and before bath, for a few minutes, with no pressure.
  5. Praise efforts, not just results. A simple sticker chart can help some children.
  6. Teach handwashing every time.

Accidents and setbacks

Accidents are part of learning. Clean up calmly without fuss or punishment. If your child is upset or resisting, it is fine to go back to nappies for a few weeks and try again later. Constipation can make potty training harder; tell your GP or health visitor if your child's poos are hard or painful.

Night-time dryness

Staying dry at night usually comes later than daytime dryness. Bedwetting is common in young children and, according to the NHS, isn't usually a concern in children under 5. Talk to your GP if it continues after 5, starts again after a long dry spell, or worries your child.

Frequently asked questions

What age should a child be potty trained?
There’s no set age. Many children show readiness between 18 months and 3 years, and girls are often ready a little earlier than boys. Following your child’s readiness usually works better than aiming for an age.
How long does potty training take?
It varies from days to months. Children who are ready often learn faster. Expect accidents, especially when tired or busy playing.
