Play & learning

Play is how they learn

You don’t need fancy toys. Talking, reading, moving and silly games do most of the work. Here are ideas for every age.

Toys that grow with them

A few open-ended toys beat a mountain of single-use ones.

Play ideas by age

  • 0–3 m

    Face-to-face chats, singing, black-and-white pictures, short tummy time on your chest or the floor.

  • 3–12 m

    Peekaboo, rattles, stacking cups, banging pots, board books, rolling a ball, splashing in the bath (supervised).

  • 1–3 y

    Push-along toys, shape sorters, chunky crayons, sand and water, dancing, simple pretend play (“tea party”).

  • 3–5 y

    Dress-up, building, play dough, puzzles, scooting and balance bikes, nature walks, counting and rhyming games.

  • 5–8 y

    Board games, Lego, crafts, cooking together, bikes, swimming, clubs and sports, reading together.

  • 8–12 y

    Hobbies, team sports, chess and strategy games, coding, music, building dens, longer books and graphic novels.

Moving more

The WHO and UK Chief Medical Officers recommend babies are active several times a day, including at least 30 minutes of tummy time spread through the day; 1 to 4 year olds at least 180 minutes of activity a day (for 3 to 4 year olds, including at least 60 minutes of energetic play); and children aged 5 and over an average of at least 60 minutes of moderate-to-vigorous activity a day.

Reading together, and free books

Sharing books every day, from birth, builds language and closeness. Free schemes include Bookstart (BookTrust, England and Wales) and Dolly Parton's Imagination Library (free books by post in participating areas of the US, UK and other countries). Your local library card is free too.

Frequently asked questions

How much screen time is OK for a 2 year old?
The WHO advises no more than 1 hour of sedentary screen time a day for 2 to 4 year olds, and less is better. The AAP suggests choosing high-quality programmes and watching together.
What are the best toys for a 1 year old?
Simple, open-ended toys: stacking cups, shape sorters, push-along toys, balls and board books. Check age labels and avoid small parts.
